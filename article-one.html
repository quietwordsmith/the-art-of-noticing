Don't You Trust Me

Written by Ndinani M

That's what God asked me. Now, let me give you some context as to how we got here. It's a normal Monday night, around 21:31. It's raining calmly outside. Everyone else is asleep, and I'm the only one awake. Reason being? I haven't eaten dinner yet, and today happens to be one of those days when everyone is free to make themselves whatever they want to eat. Realistically, whatever I make is something everyone else would be drooling over if I prepared it while they were awake, especially the little ones. Hence, I'm only busy around the kitchen xa indim ndedwa ohleliyo. I've filled the kettle with water and turned it on. I've grabbed some sugar, tea, a teaspoon and my palm-sized cup, placing everything on the kitchen island. I've warmed up isonka sam sombhako in the microwave, and I have McCain Skinny Fries baking in the oven. I'm contemplating adding something else, but I decided to clean my colon today, so simplicity is KEY during such times. Somewhere between preparing food and making my tea, I say: "God, I don't want to be in any relationship anytime soon." Now, with my conversations with God, I always let out a "hear me out" literally out loud, or simply carry on talking whenever I'm discussing something sensitive. It might be a bad habit, it might not be, but I tend to ramble a lot when talking to God. I'm mos trying to get my point across, or explain my reasons, even though I know He already knows. There's something endearing about telling Him things He already knows. Anywho. I tell Him how exhausted I am from previous experiences. How I've repeatedly relied on the benefit of the doubt, convincing myself to see things through even when I've noticed the little signs that the Holy Spirit has helped me pick up on. The signs that essentially say, "Sisi, it's time to leave ngoku."
